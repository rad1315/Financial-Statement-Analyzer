# Financial Statement Analysis: Apple Inc. (AAPL)
**Author:** Richard Daley  
**Date:** September 2026  
**Subject:** Phase 1 Ratio & Capital Structure Evaluation  

## 1. Executive Summary
This memorandum analyzes the financial health, liquidity, and operational efficiency of Apple Inc. (AAPL) using multi-year data pulled directly from SEC EDGAR filings via automated script execution. The analysis evaluates core performance indicators across liquidity, profitability, leverage, and asset management.

## 2. Key Findings & Ratio Interpretation

### Liquidity & Solvency
* **Current Ratio:** Apple’s current ratio historically hovers below traditional textbook benchmarks (often under 1.0). While standard corporate finance theory suggests a current ratio above 1.0 is ideal for short-term safety, Apple’s efficient working capital management, massive operating cash flow generation, and strong short-term investment portfolio allow it to comfortably service short-term obligations without holding excess idle cash.
* **Debt-to-Equity:** Apple maintains a leveraged capital structure through strategic debt issuance to fund share repurchases and dividends. The resulting debt-to-equity ratio reflects an optimized capital deployment strategy rather than financial distress, anchored by stable equity and massive earnings power.

### Profitability & Returns
* **Net Profit Margin:** Apple demonstrates elite pricing power and brand premium, maintaining robust net profit margins consistently. This high margin profile is a core driver of its exceptional economic moat.
* **Return on Assets (ROA) & Return on Equity (ROE):** Apple exhibits exceptionally high ROE values. Because the company aggressively returns capital to shareholders via buybacks (which reduces total stockholders' equity on the balance sheet), its net income is divided over a smaller equity base, magnifying ROE while maintaining strong asset productivity.

### Operational Efficiency
* **Asset Turnover:** Asset turnover remains steady, reflecting how effectively Apple utilizes its asset base (including retail infrastructure, supply chain investments, and intellectual property) to generate top-line revenue. 

## 3. Methodological Note & Data Integrity
Data was ingested natively from SEC XBRL feeds. XBRL fallback mapping protocols were utilized to normalize line-item variations (such as historical revenue tags shifting under ASC 606 standards) to ensure multi-year comparability without manual spreadsheet intervention.

To contextualize these findings, Phase 2 of this project will expand the script to ingest competing tech sector filers (e.g., Microsoft, Google) to benchmark cross-company capital structures and margin variations.
